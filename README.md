# swift-acoustics

Use this package when a sound should come from a thing that vibrates and is struck, computed a sample at a time from its own equations: a drumhead, a bar, a bell, a soundboard. `Acoustics.Resonator` is a set of resonances that a `Acoustics.Striker`, a mass on a felt that stiffens as it is pressed, can hit: the two stay in contact for as long as the motion keeps them there, so how long they touch, how the striker comes back and how bright the stroke is all come out of the stepping. `Acoustics.Membrane` is a round head under tension and gives the numbers such a resonator needs: each mode's frequency under the load of the air, its shape, its mass, how much of it a patch of felt feels, how it stretches the head, and what it radiates in a direction. Quantities are `Double`s in SI units: metres, kilograms, seconds, newtons, hertz, pascals. This package holds no instrument: which head, which felt and which measured numbers make a kettledrum is an instrument's business. A room and its microphones are not here either.

## Laws

- A resonance is a mass on a spring with a loss: `frequency` in hertz, `decay` in nepers a second (its level falls 60 dB in 6.908 / decay seconds), `mass` in kilograms, and `reach`, how far the place where it is struck moves when the resonance is displaced by one. `stretch` and `heard` are zero and empty unless given.
- `advance()` moves everything one `interval` on by the centred difference of each resonance's equation, with its stiffness warped to (2 / interval) sin(π frequency × interval) squared, so that a resonance with no loss rings at exactly its frequency, whatever the interval; the interval must be shorter than one over π times the highest frequency.
- A stroke: `strike(with:at:)` puts a striker at the surface, moving into it at a speed. While it presses, its force is stiffness × compression to the power of its exponent, and zero when it does not; the force of each step is the change in the felt's stored energy over the change in compression across two steps, found by 64 halvings, so the same stroke gives the same samples everywhere. When the striker has left the surface and moves away it is gone, `struck` is false, and it takes its motion with it.
- `energy`, the motion and the springs' and the felt's stored energy together, never grows. With no loss it stays the same through a whole stroke, to rounding, until the striker is gone.
- A resonator made with a `stiffening` holds its head tighter the further it moves: every resonance's stiffness rises by stiffening × its `stretch` × the sum over all resonances of stretch × displacement squared, taken with the mean of the displacement a step before and a step after. Energy is kept as before, and a head that moves far rings sharper.
- `heard` has one value for each ear, as many as the fewest any mode carries: the sum over the resonances of `moving` × its acceleration less `turning` × 2π frequency × its velocity, both taken at the step before the newest. `moving` is the part of a resonance's sound in step with its acceleration and `turning` the part a quarter of a cycle behind.
- `displace(to:moving:)` sets the resonances' displacements and velocities, in their order; those not named keep theirs and a velocity not given is zero.
- `surface` is the displacement at the place that is struck, `touching` says whether the striker pressed during the last step, and `force` is what it pressed with.
- A membrane's mode has `diameters` and `circles`, the lines that stay still across it and around it (the rim counts as a circle), and `zero`, the zero of the Bessel function that sets it. `modes(below:in:)` gives every mode sounding under a frequency, by diameters and then circles. Each mode with diameters stands for two, a quarter of a turn apart around the head.
- A mode's `frequency(of:in:)` is its wavenumber, zero / radius, times √(tension / moved mass) over 2π. The moved mass per area is the head's `density` plus `load` × the air's density / wavenumber: the air that moves with the head, as a first approximation in one fitted number. With no load it is the head in a vacuum. With a load of 3.95 the head measured by Christian and others in 1984 (0.32 m, 5360 N/m, 0.262 kg/m²; as given by Packer, Durham thesis 1993, tables 7.1 and 7.2) has its (1,1) mode at 172 Hz and the three above it within 3.5% of the measured 1.50, 1.97 and 2.44 times that.
- `mass(of:in:)` is the moved mass per area times the integral of the squared shape over the head; `shape(of:at:)` is the shape along a radius where it is largest around the head, and zero outside the rim; `patch(of:across:)` is how much of the mode a round patch of that radius feels against a point, 2 J₁(wavenumber × radius) over wavenumber × radius; `stretch(of:)` is the integral of the squared slope of the shape.
- `beam(of:at:tilted:in:)` is the far sound pressure times distance for a unit acceleration of the mode, at an angle from straight above the head, for a head set in a wall (Rayleigh's integral), in the direction around the head where the mode is loudest. A listener at another angle around hears it times the cosine of diameters × that angle, and a quarter of a cycle later for each diameter. Straight above, only modes without diameters are heard.
- `radiated(by:at:in:)` is the decay a mode has from the sound it sends out alone, its power over the half space above over twice its energy, by 200 slices of the angle. For the head above it gives ring times within a factor of two of the ones Christian and others calculated for the first two modes with one circle and the first two with two (Packer's table 6.1).
- `Acoustics.Air.room` is air at 20 °C: 1.204 kg/m³ and 343.2 m/s.
